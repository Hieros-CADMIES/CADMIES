# The Treaty of the Decentralized Commons

A Social Contract for an Interoperable, Resilient, and People-Governed Web

## Preamble

We, the builders, archivists, researchers, and stewards of decentralized infrastructure, recognize that:

The internet was designed as an open, distributed network, yet power has concentrated in a handful of corporate platforms

Decentralized technologies (storage, compute, naming, identity) exist but remain fragmented, each operating as its own silo

No single project can decentralize the entire web alone

The barrier is not technical—it is social coordination

We therefore establish this Treaty to create a common foundation for interoperability, shared standards, and community governance.

This is not a legal document. It is a social contract. Its enforcement comes not from courts, but from the collective will of the community. Those who violate its principles will find themselves ignored, incompatible, and irrelevant.

## Article I: Principles

Interoperability First — No project shall lock data, identity, or functionality behind proprietary walls. All systems must speak common protocols.

Interest-Neutral Stewardship — The coordination of the commons shall be managed by a Steward (or body of Stewards) with no financial or competitive stake in any protocol or platform.

People Govern — Governance is by rough consensus of active participants. One person, one voice. No token-weighting. No corporate veto.

Transparency — All decisions, meetings, budgets, and disputes shall be public and auditable.

Forkability — Any group that disagrees with the direction of the commons may fork the treaty, the standards, or the implementation. The best fork wins by adoption, not by force.

Iterative Evolution — The treaty is never finished. It grows, adapts, and improves through use, feedback, and formal revision.

Preservation of History — The commons shall prioritize the permanent, tamper-resistant preservation of public knowledge.

## Article II: Technical Commitments

All signatories agree to adopt and implement the following standards, or to work toward their adoption in good faith.

2.1 Content Addressing
All data shall be addressed by its content, using CIDs (Content Identifiers) as defined by the IPFS specification (or an equivalent interoperable standard)

This ensures that data is location-independent and verifiable

2.2 Naming and Lookup
The commons shall maintain an interoperable naming and discovery system, building on the research of the IRTF's DINRG

This may include DHTs, ENS, Handshake, or other decentralized naming systems—as long as they are interoperable

2.3 Storage
Signatories shall support at least one decentralized storage protocol (IPFS, Filecoin, Arweave, Storj, or equivalent) for public data

Data shall be replicated across at least three independent nodes or networks

2.4 Retrieval
All data shall be retrievable via common retrieval protocols (bitswap, graphsync, or equivalent)

The commons shall maintain a shared retrieval index or network

2.5 Identity
Signatories shall support decentralized identity (DIDs, UCANs, or equivalent)

Identity shall be portable—users own their identity, not the platform

2.6 Economic Incentives
The commons shall develop shared primitives for rewarding node operators, storage providers, and contributors

These primitives shall be interoperable across projects, avoiding the "every project reinvents the wheel" problem

## Article III: Governance

3.1 The Steward
A Steward (or body of Stewards) shall be appointed to coordinate the commons.

Duties:

Convene meetings and summits

Maintain the charter and standards documents

Facilitate dispute resolution

Report transparently on all activities

Maintain a public registry of signatories

Restrictions:

The Steward shall have no financial stake in any protocol, platform, or token

The Steward shall not favor one project over another

The Steward shall serve a fixed term (e.g., 2 years) and may be renewed by community vote

Removal: The Steward may be removed by a supermajority vote (75%) of active participants.

3.2 Decision-Making
Rough Consensus — Decisions are made by rough consensus, not unanimity. Sustained opposition blocks a decision; mere disagreement does not.

Voting — When formal votes are needed, all active participants (individuals, not organizations) have one vote

Corporate Participation — Corporations and large organizations may participate, but they receive no special voting weight

3.3 Amendments
Amendments to this Treaty shall be proposed in writing

An open comment period of at least 6 months shall follow

Ratification requires rough consensus of active participants

No amendment shall violate the core principles of interoperability, neutrality, or forkability

## Article IV: Dispute Resolution

4.1 Technical Disputes
When two implementations disagree on a standard, the community shall convene a technical working group to resolve the issue

The working group's recommendation shall be published for comment

If consensus cannot be reached, both implementations may coexist until the community converges on one

4.2 Governance Disputes
If a Steward is accused of bias or misconduct, the community shall investigate

If the accusation is substantiated, the Steward shall be removed

No legal action shall be pursued—the enforcement is social

4.3 Violations of the Treaty
A signatory that violates the Treaty shall be publicly identified

Other signatories may choose to cease interoperability with the violator

The violator may regain standing by correcting the violation and making amends

## Article V: Funding

5.1 Shared Funding Pool
Signatories may contribute to a shared fund to support:

Steward operations

Infrastructure (lookup servers, indexers, archival nodes)

Grants for interoperability work

Legal defense (if needed) for the commons

5.2 No Profit
The Steward shall not generate profit from the commons

Any funds raised shall be used solely for the operation and maintenance of the commons

All budgets shall be public

5.3 Independent Audits
The commons shall undergo annual financial and technical audits

Audits shall be published publicly

## Article VI: Ratification

6.1 Signatories
Any project, organization, or individual that agrees to the principles and technical commitments of this Treaty may become a Signatory

Signatories shall be listed in a public registry maintained by the Steward

6.2 Initial Ratification
This Treaty shall be open for public comment for a period of 12 months

After 12 months, it shall be ratified by rough consensus of the community

Ratification shall be indicated by a public declaration, not by legal signature

6.3 Ongoing Ratification
New Signatories may join at any time by public declaration

The Treaty shall be reviewed every 3 years for relevance and revised as needed

## Article VII: The Future

7.1 Vision
A web where data lives on the commons, not in corporate silos

A web where users own their identity and control their data

A web that persists for decades, immune to censorship and corporate failure

7.2 Commitment
We commit to building this web, piece by piece, standard by standard, conversation by conversation

We commit to patience, persistence, and good faith

We commit to putting the commons above any single project, company, or ideology
